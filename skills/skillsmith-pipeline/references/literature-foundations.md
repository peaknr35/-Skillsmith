# Research foundations for Skillsmith (verified against PDFs)

Every claim and number below was checked directly against the downloaded arXiv PDFs (`[[pdf p. N]]`). Where a widely circulated summary or another AI overstated or conflated a paper's claims, the correction is noted explicitly.

## 1. Formal definition, Triad gate, and 9-stage lifecycle — *A Systematic Survey of Agentic Skills: Architecture, Lifecycle, and Security* ([arXiv:2608.29596v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29596))
- **Formal six-tuple (verified, pdf p. 2, Eq. 1):**
  $$s = (\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{I}, \mathcal{C}, \mathcal{T}, \pi, \mathcal{E})$$
  where $\mathcal{A}$ is the activation condition, $\mathcal{I}$ procedural instructions or guidance, $\mathcal{C}$ applicability constraints and environment preconditions, $\mathcal{T}$ accessible tools and interfaces, $\pi$ the execution policy or procedural control flow, and $\mathcal{E}$ intended state transitions or post-condition effects.
- **Triad membership criteria (verified, pdf p. 2):**
  1. *Encapsulated Modularity*: discrete, externalized package bundling procedural logic, $\mathcal{T}$, and $\mathcal{C}$ that can be authored, versioned, validated, and updated without altering model weights or global harness code.
  2. *Late-Binding Dynamic Invocation*: loaded on demand via conditional evaluation of $\mathcal{A}(x_t, g) \to \{0, 1\}$ over current state $x_t$ and goal $g$.
  3. *Procedural State Transformation*: dictates how to execute a task and handle errors through policy $\pi$, rather than recording declarative facts or chronological histories.
  - **How Skillsmith uses this:** Stage 02 (`schema.SourceMap.triad`) requires all three booleans to be `true` before any proposed skill can use `artifact: "execution_skill"`. Otherwise the pipeline downgrades the artifact to `decision_guide` or `evidence_reference`, or abstains (`actionable: false`).
- **Nine-stage lifecycle (verified, pdf p. 1):** (1) autonomous discovery, (2) authoring and representation formats, (3) memory storage, (4) dynamic retrieval and routing, (5) composition and orchestration, (6) execution and repair, (7) lifelong adaptation, (8) empirical evaluation, and (9) security governance.

## 2. Admission and compilation — *SkillAlchemy: Open-World Agent Skill Creation* ([arXiv:2608.23417v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23417))
- **Three-stage formulation (verified, pdf p. 2–5, Eq. 2):**
  $$(g, \mathcal{S}) \xrightarrow{\Phi_1} (Q, F) \xrightarrow{\Phi_2} (R_g, R_e) \xrightarrow{\Phi_3, \mathcal{C}} A = \langle \text{SKILL.md}, X \rangle$$
  - $\Phi_1$ (*Implicit Requirement Discovery*, pdf p. 3–4): maps an underspecified brief $g$ to operational factors and uses **contrastive probes** $h = \langle d, x, x' \rangle$ (substitution, boundary, and neighbor probes) across four behavioral components $K = \{c, a, r, v\}$ (conditions, action, recovery, verification). A factor becomes an implicit requirement only when evidence shows $\text{Treat}_k(x) \neq \text{Treat}_k(x')$ (Eq. 3).
  - $\Phi_2$ (*Evidence-Grounded Procedure Admission*, pdf p. 4–5): groups findings by the decision they inform (`Align(F)`) rather than source section, and evaluates three predicates per candidate $\pi_j$: *Supported* ($S_j$), *Consistent* ($C_j$), and *Reusable* ($U_j$, justified beyond a single source-local case). Admission rule (Eq. 5):
    - $\text{General}$ ($S_j \land C_j \land U_j$) $\to R_g$ (compiled as reusable instructions in `SKILL.md`),
    - $\text{Scoped}$ ($S_j \land C_j \land \neg U_j$) $\to R_e$ (retained as context-bound examples/notes in `references/`),
    - $\text{Exclude}$ (otherwise; kept in the audit record, omitted from the skill).
  - $\Phi_3$ (*Grammar-Guided Compilation*, pdf p. 5, 12, Table A.2): organizes admitted content without adding procedures, broadening scope, or inventing verification steps. Median top-level `SKILL.md` length across configurations is **144–157 lines** (pdf p. 10, Fig. A.1; note: the `<= 500 lines` cap in Skillsmith comes from Anthropic's Claude Code skill guidelines, not from these preprints).
- **Results (verified, pdf p. 1):** Across 87 SkillsBench v1.1 tasks, SkillAlchemy improves pass rate by **+19.9 percentage points** over no-skill execution and **+8.6 percentage points** over the strongest automated baseline.

## 3. Source representation — *Do Agents Need a New Kind of Paper? SPRA: Structured Paper Representation for Agents* ([arXiv:2610.21424v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21424))
- **What SPRA actually builds (verified, pdf p. 1):** An Empirical-AI profile organizing **Problems, Contributions, Components, ExperimentSetups, Measures, and Findings**, linked by typed internal relations, citation relations (`builds_on`, `uses_component`, `compares_with`), and source locators for verification across >1M AI papers (**19.95M research units**, **24.07M internal relations**).
- **Nuance check on numbers (verified, pdf p. 1):** SPRA improves source-conditioned scientific QA by **+0.381/5 over passage retrieval**, while remaining **0.078/5 lower than Full Compact** (full paper text) at **95.3% fewer input+output tokens** than Full Compact and **28.5% fewer** than passage retrieval. In other words, structured extraction beats naive chunk retrieval on quality and cuts tokens by 95% vs dumping the whole paper, rather than beating full-text on raw score at any cost.

## 4. Process vs completion rubrics — *SkillForgeBench: Evaluating Agent Reliability Grounded in Agent Skill Specifications* ([arXiv:2610.14918v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14918))
- **What SkillForgeBench actually tests (verified, pdf p. 1):** Translates skill-embedded requirements across **842 sandboxed tasks** into **completion rubrics** (final outputs) and **process rubrics** (intermediate step compliance).
- **Key empirical finding (verified, pdf p. 1):** Individual rubric pass rates are **70.0% to 85.7%**, but **strict completion** (satisfying *all* constraints simultaneously) drops to **12.6% to 40.9%** (led by Opus 4.8 at 40.9%). Agents fail process rubrics far more often than completion rubrics (under **3%** process pass rate for Haiku 4.5). Top-tier models gain much more from skills than smaller models ($\Delta 19.2\%$ for Opus 4.8 vs $\Delta 5.8\%$ for Haiku 4.5).
- **Correction of a common misattribution:** Trigger tests with near-miss distractors and neutral-task A/B testing are **Skillsmith's own stage-06 design**, not from SkillForgeBench. What we adopted from SkillForgeBench is `process_checks` and **strict completion** scoring in `evalrun.py`.

## 5. Exposure bias in text skills — *Procedural Exposure Bias in Skill-Augmented Language Model Agents* ([arXiv:2610.26062v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26062))
- **Actual definition (verified, pdf p. 1):** Procedural Exposure Bias (PEB) is the systematic tendency of language models to **increasingly favor an action as its associated procedural pattern is repeatedly exposed in a skill, even when the interaction state and the action's relevance remain unchanged**. (It is *not* merely "following stylistic form while drifting from math constraints.")
- **Mitigation reported by the paper (verified, pdf p. 1):** Rendering procedural content **visually** attenuates exposure-induced preference shifts compared to text and improves macro-average task success on ALFWorld, ScienceWorld, and WebShop across all four evaluated models.
- **How Skillsmith mitigates PEB in text skills:** Because `SKILL.md` is textual, `_StepDedup` in `compile.py` collapses duplicate action steps across units (`Same as u01 step 1`) so merging units does not multiply the exposure frequency of a shared step, and `verify-report.md` / `skillsmith audit` report repeated step patterns.

## 6. Selective exposure and skill routing — *SkillBrowser* ([arXiv:2610.23554v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23554)) & *SKALOS* ([arXiv:2610.34728v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34728))
- **SkillBrowser (verified, pdf p. 1):** Progressive disclosure of a growing skill catalog still causes **context pollution** from metadata, while static initial-query retrieval misses capability needs that emerge mid-reasoning. SkillBrowser uses a Meta-Skill to query and load skills on demand across 6 SRA-Bench subsets, noting **under-invocation of useful skills and extra latency** as trade-offs.
- **SKALOS (verified, pdf p. 1, 6):** Distinguishes similar skills using a capability graph and semiring-based **capability witness learning** (scoring each branch by its weakest evidence and checking joint support within one implementation). Improves SkillRet **NDCG@10 by +2.68 pp** over a matched text-only reranker (and **+3.70 pp** over Full `SKILL.md` reranking, pdf p. 11) and raises ALFWorld task success from **22% to 29%**.
- **How Skillsmith uses this:** `mcp_server.py` exposes `match_skills(situation)`, which ranks skills across the library by unit-level capability witnesses (matched units and scores) rather than dumping all `SKILL.md` files into context.

## 7. Multimodal extraction & persistent evolution — *RESOURCE2SKILL* ([arXiv:2606.29538v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29538)), *WikiSkill* ([arXiv:2608.27454v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27454)), and *How Agent Skills Evolve* ([arXiv:2611.04456v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04456))
- **RESOURCE2SKILL (verified, pdf p. 1):** Organizes skills from videos, repos, and articles into a hierarchical Skill Wiki combining text, code, visual examples, metadata, and provenance (**+11.9 pp** over no-skill agents across 7 domains; wins **26 of 28** model–domain cells).
- **WikiSkill (verified, pdf p. 1, 5, 28):** Separates raw execution experience, a persistent knowledge wiki, and executable skills (`SKILL.md` + `PURPOSE.md` mapping the skill back to its motivating wiki pattern). Shows that evolved skills transfer across model families.
- **How Agent Skills Evolve on GitHub (verified, pdf p. 1):** Studies **84,200 skills** and **750,898 commits** on GitHub. Five findings: (i) reliability gets too little attention during skill edits (motivating execution-contract design with verification/recovery); (ii) edits concentrate in project-coupled skills; (iii) changes are mostly incremental enrichment; (iv) context redundancy is a major maintenance issue; (v) security edits often fail to reduce risk without regression testing.
- **How Skillsmith uses this:** Stage 05 writes `references/provenance.json` (source SHA-256, Skillsmith version, and per-unit lineage from stage-03 IDs, pages, and evidence grades) alongside `SKILL.md`, and `skillsmith audit` / `eval-run` / `eval-quality` serve as regression tests when `_config/` prompts or sources are updated.

## 8. Context scoping — *Interpretable Context Methodology (ICM)* ([arXiv:2603.16021v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16021))
- **Verified in `runs/icm-paper/stages/01_ingest/output/pages.jsonl` (pdf p. 5, 7, 8):** Layers 0–2 contribute ~1,300–1,600 tokens, Layer 3 adds 500–2,000 tokens, and total context delivered at any stage typically ranges from **2,000 to 8,000 tokens** versus **>40,000 tokens** in a monolithic prompt.
